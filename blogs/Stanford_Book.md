title: Stanford's Unique Admit Package
date: 2026-05-20
---

Instead of giving me an admit package with an admittance letter, a cute banner, and a couple stickers like every other college, Stanford gave me a book titled "You Got In! Now What? 100 Insights into Finding Your Best Life in College" by James T. Hamitlon. This is possibly the most Stanford thing ever. So in honor of that and finishing the book here are my favorite chapters.

- Chapter 1 "Pursue an education, not just the degree"
  - Self explanatory
- Chapter 4 "Life is short"
  - Because life moves fast, move with urgency. Also includes quotes from two of amazing entrepeuners Steve Jobs and Phil Knight
- Chapter 11 "Stay Hungry. Stay Foolish"
  - No explanation
- Chapter 12 "Be Here Now."
  - Relevent to how fast we tend to focus on goals (the future) when the present is something to savor
- Chapter 18 "And A History."
  - I have always been a history lover. Seeing Stanford's individual singling of history as the only subject out of the STEM or Humanities is nice as someone who loves the subject. It's ability to have stories, figures, and people who parallel our lives is nice.
- Chapter 22 "Keep a Failure Resume"
  - I have many flaws as a person. But, that makes it ever more exciting to work on them to be better. It's nice to see Stanford take a drastic approach towards accountability where we literally write down our flaws. It's nice to see great figures - professors and leaders - do the same as well.
- Chapter 26 "Bias Toward Action is the New *Carpe Diem*" 
  - "Don't chase the allures of money and comfort... Try something new, and then try it again"
- Chapter 26 "Be Curious"
  - A great motto
- Chapter 36 "Act Like A Start-Up"
  - Probably the most Stanford phrase in the world. The main idea is being flexible enough to pivot and adjust to hardships. I like this idea a lot
- Chapter 39 "Life is not linear"
  - Duh, but hard to grasp until you look backwards
- Chapter 47 "Do Nothing"
  - Movement/Action does not mean productivity. Putting our phones down, pausing from the chase of a career, and looking around to admire the world around
- Chapter 48 "Eat, Sleep, Exercise"
  - Essentials. I love the emphasis here.
- Chapter 50 "Your Friends Will Show Up In Your Highlight Reel"
  - We are human and we crave connection. I agree
- Chapter 51 "Create Time For Important Relationships"
  - Agreed. Know who you care about deeply and choose *wisely* about who you let into your truly deep circle (I made this mistake once).
- Chapter 52 "Be Open To Love"
  - I suppose
- Chapter 59 "Go Global"
  - I plan to
- Chapter 60 "Go To Office Hours"
  - It brought up really good perspectives from a professor stand point. The conversations that aren't related to an assignment can spark the greatest insights.
- Chapter 63 "Ask Questions"
  - Everyone
- Chapter 67 "Identity Can Take a Toll"
  - Like 6 7, your identity and meaning can change because a lot of things we see as grounded are truly abstract.
- Chapter 71 "Read a Book. Read a Mind"
  - Books provide a crucial role in expanding a person's mind
- Chapter 72 "Seeing Others See You is a Burden"
  - It provided a really unique insight on how expectations become a burden through a unique story
- Chapter 79 "Don't Free Ride"
  - This is something that I still struggle with. I need to be better at organizing and planning stuff, taking that first step.
- Chapter 84 "Universities are Imperfect"
  - Wow. I never thought Stanford would admit this.
- Chapther 86 "Don't... Simply Follow the Market"
  - I didn't expect this chapter to be a condemnation of social media. And, I completely agree.
- Chapter 87 "Don't... Maximize Lifetime Income"
  - I really liked the idea of how Stanford ditches the idea of money being the end all. It really shouldn't be the driver of what we pursue.
- Chapther 98 "Dont... Get Serious"
  - A couple laughs means a lot in life
- Chapther 99 "Don't... Ignore Your Ancestors"
  - Really cool to see the acknowledgment of the past. In fact, one of my favorite quotes is in here: "[Julian Castro] The American Dream is not a sprint, or even a marathon, but a relay." Like damn.
- Chapter 100 "Don't... Put It Off"
  - Yes

*Honorable mention of a banger quote "Many lessons in this book come from personal narratives about hte impact of college. These bear out the insight that life is lived forawrd but understood backward."
Goddamn. There are some bars.

Overall, I'm really happy I read it and I choose Stanford. It seems like the mindset and ideals already match with me.
